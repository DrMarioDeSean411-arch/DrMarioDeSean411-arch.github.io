## Philosophy

Education is a right. It isn't a privilege, and I don't treat it like one. That belief drives how I teach technology and computing: hard material should be reachable for every student, whatever they walked in with. Three principles hold my teaching together. Access to knowledge for everyone. Learning through real-world application. A classroom where different perspectives get real room.

Learning by doing sits at the center. I came to the classroom from systems management, network security, and IT consultation, and my projects mirror situations students will meet on the job. In my graduate cybersecurity courses, students work through actual case studies of security breaches, run forensic analyses, and build mitigation strategies. The theory sticks better when they have to use it.

I build in layers. Foundations first, then harder applications, so that by the time the work gets sophisticated students have the confidence to handle it.

Inclusion matters a great deal in technology education. I choose examples that connect with students from different backgrounds, and I've taught both traditional and non-traditional students, which taught me to adjust method to learning style. Engagement takes several forms in my classes, from small-group discussion to anonymous polling, so everyone has a way into the conversation.

## Assessment

Tests alone don't tell me enough. I use authentic assessment: projects that simulate real scenarios and force students to combine skills and solve problems. Regular feedback sessions show students where they stand and what needs work.

## Course development

Industry relevance and sound educational practice guide how I build courses. I've designed and taught in cybersecurity and network management at the graduate level, covering security operations, breach response, digital forensics, penetration testing, CISSP preparation, and intrusion detection. The same goes for data science and visualization, cloud computing (Azure and AWS Academy), IT fundamentals, and CompTIA certification preparation. Technology keeps changing, so I update my curriculum with emerging work in cloud computing, generative AI, and advanced cybersecurity practice. To reach different learning styles I use interactive demonstrations and collaborative problem-solving activities.

## Commitment to student success

Nothing beats watching students turn into confident, capable professionals who can adapt as technology keeps shifting under them. Educators owe them more than technical skill. We also owe them ethical awareness and a sense of social responsibility. Because I've worked as a technology professional, I know the skills employers look for, and I mentor, advise, and coach for career preparedness with that in mind.
